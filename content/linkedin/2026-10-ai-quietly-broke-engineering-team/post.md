I spent the first half of this year chasing an AI speed-up that never came.

Senior leadership expected AI to make our development several times faster. Other teams spun up swarms of agents and built "AI factories". We tried too. For us, it didn't really help.

I believed in that speed-up too, or at least I wanted to. When we couldn't reach it, I got anxious, and then burned out.

Mid-year, I started to look at where our time actually went. Code was no longer the blocker. Deciding what to build was, and the integration work around it. We had been measuring the wrong thing.

So I looked closer at my team. After three months of thinking, these are my observations and intuitions about what AI quietly changed in it.

Code got cheap, and we lost our easiest reason to say no. When building was expensive, we had to choose. Now every idea arrives as a working demo, and every demo waits for a decision.

I watched experienced engineers lose the room. AI makes less experienced people sound sure of themselves. Seniors still say "it depends", because they know what usually breaks. Leaders like confidence. Guess who gets heard, and who fixes it later.

And people stopped asking each other for help. The SQL question, the broken pipeline, the "can you look at this?" now go to a chat window. It's faster. But those small questions were how we got to know each other. You fixed someone's query and ended up talking about the tennis racket behind their chair.

None of this shows up in a sprint report. That's what makes it so easy to miss.

I'm still positive about AI, and I think we have to adapt. But it's hard to adapt to something you haven't noticed.

What stopped happening in your team this year that no metric would catch?
