By the end of my long Claude Code sessions, Claude had lost most of what I told it at the start.

Only 1 in 10 early facts was still fully there.

I measured it.

My evenings go to a solo research project. One session can run for hours: an idea, some code, an experiment, a new idea, and again.

When a session gets too long, Claude Code swaps it for a short summary. One of mine went from 782,000 tokens to 13,000 in one step. Later, the summary gets summarised again.

With Claude's help, I picked 72 early facts from five long sessions and checked which ones Claude could still see:
• after the first summary: 69%
• at the end: 10%

So I built Cogi, and kept the idea simple:
• keep every message word for word on my laptop
• search it when needed
• no AI call to save a message, so it costs nothing

When Claude asks, it gets back a 1,000-token budget of my own words, about one page.

When I asked directly about an early fact, that one page fully answered 79% of the questions.

Then I compared Cogi with four other free memory plugins on LongMemEval_M. It has 500 questions, and each answer is hidden in about 1.2 million tokens of old chats. Every plugin used its best published setting.

How often the right chat was in the top five:
• Cogi: 93.0%
• hippo-memory: 89.8%
• deja-vu: 89.0%
• MemPalace: 87.8%
• agentmemory: 86.6%

Finding the right chat is not enough. Claude needs the right text in what it reads.

With the same 2,500-token budget for every plugin (about two and a half pages), each on its default settings, how often everything the answer needed was there:
• Cogi: 70.6%
• MemPalace: 61.2%
• the other three: under 25%, mostly because they return a whole old chat or a few short snippets, not because their search is worse

On a second benchmark, LoCoMo, Cogi's 1,000-token page was 95% as good as rereading the whole conversation, with 9 times less text to read.

Some honest limits:
• I ran these tests myself, and the plugins' authors have not checked them
• the tests use chats, not coding sessions
• Cogi works best when you ask Claude to check its memory

Often, the beauty is in a simple idea that just works. I like those.
